1 · Problem Analysis

1.1 The scenario that breaks today

An ASHA worker in a village ~18 km from the PHC meets a pregnant woman with blurred vision and swelling — preeclampsia red flags. She has no triage protocol, no phone path to the Medical Officer, and the referral is a verbal "go to the CHC": untracked, unverified, unremembered. If the woman never reaches the CHC, no system anywhere notices.

1.2 What happens today, and what it costs

1.3 Why this persists

This is not a technology gap — it is an accountability gap. Every deployed digital tool is a vertical silo owned by one cadre or one tier (teleconsults by eSanjeevani's hubs, ANM reporting by ANMOL/RCH, drugs by DVDMS). Nobody's system closes the loop across sub-centre → PHC → CHC → district hospital, so no actor can see — or be answerable for — a referral dying in transit. Section 2 documents this silo-by-silo with sources.

1.4 Measurable success criteria

  • Referral completion rate: target +20–30% over facility baseline within pilot cohort
  • Median time-to-first-consultation for triaged high-risk cases
  • Follow-up adherence % for enrolled maternal/child/NCD cohorts
  • Stock-visibility accuracy (reported vs verified stock match rate) and staleness distribution
  • Sync success rate over ≤2G links; median data-freshness per device

2 · Existing Systems Landscape (with documented gaps)

Method: for each incumbent we state what it covers, its scale, and the specific gap our design attacks — citing official dashboards, peer-reviewed analysis, or government workshop records where possible.

2.1 eSanjeevani — national telemedicine service

43+ crore consultations (Nov 2025, Rajya Sabha reply) ~1.1 lakh AB-HWC spokes · 14,188 hubs (2022 figures) Hub-and-spoke doctor-to-doctor consults facilitated by CHOs at Health & Wellness Centres, plus direct-to-patient eSanjeevani OPD.

Documented gaps (PMC11422547 — "Reimagining India's National Telemedicine Service"): analysis of 60M+ consultations found lack of screening tools, incorrect OPD recommendations, unstandardized free-text symptom recording in referrals, wrong-specialty referrals with inadequate details, over-referral (Jharkhand study); consultants cannot track past treatment progression or access prior records; a consultation "ends as an isolated single-step event"; no feedback loop to the referring health worker; no publicly available triage SOPs or competency governance. The paper explicitly calls for SOP-guided triage pathways, structured referral support, and feedback loops.

2.2 ABDM — Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission rails

~96.85 crore ABHA numbers (Aug 2026) 113.7 crore linked health records 5.58 lakh facilities registered Provides registries (HFR/HPR/drug registry), consent-artifact flows, and FHIR-R4-based record sharing through HIP/HIU gateways.

Gap: adoption ≠ integration. Of 3,041 active sandbox integrators, only 545 have completed successful integration (NHA dashboard, Aug 2026). Production HIP certification is a months-long compliance process — any credible solution must run on local identifiers with progressive ABHA linking behind a swappable adapter, not hard-depend on certified ABDM flows from day one.

2.3 Kilkari & Mobile Academy (MoHFW, implemented by ARMMAN)

Kilkari: 21M+ women/children reached Mobile Academy: 166k FLWs trained Weekly time-appropriate IVR audio messages to mothers from 2nd trimester to child age 1, auto-subscribed from the MCTS/RCH registry; Mobile Academy delivers IVR training to frontline workers.

Gap: one-way education. Calls are driven by registry stage (LMP/DOB), not by actual encounters, risk status, or referrals. A missed ANC visit does not change the call plan; there is no worker-facing action, no escalation, no closure signal back into the health system.

2.4 Khushi Baby CHIP — closest non-governmental competitor

85,000+ community health workers enabled 60M+ people tracked Offline-first CHW mobile platform covering RMNCAH + NCD + TB data entry, deployed as nodal technical partner to the Rajasthan Department of Health (also Maharashtra, Karnataka); markets itself on "closing care loops at the last mile."

Honest assessment: this is the nearest living implementation of our vision and must not be dismissed. Structural differences available to us: (a) KB is an NGO delivery partner tied to specific state deployments, whereas an open-source, ABDM-sandbox-native reference architecture is portable across states and auditable by any implementer; (b) KB's strength is program-wise data collection; a first-class referral-SLA state machine with acknowledged escalation, cross-tier stock visibility, and teleconsultation routing as one integrated loop is not their documented center of gravity. Our demo must out-execute theirs on the loop, not the forms.

2.5 ANMOL / RCH portal

Cadre-specific ANM reporting application feeding the RCH portal (name-based tracking of ANC/PNC/immunization). Gap: online-only usage pattern, ANM-workflow-bound, no referral tracking, no teleconsultation, no stock visibility; ASHAs remain on paper.

2.6 DVDMS / e-Aushadhi — drug logistics

60,927 facilities onboarded (DVDMS global stats) Warehouse-to-facility procurement, indent, and inventory tracking; state variants (e-Aushadhi in UP/Rajasthan).

Gaps (NHSRC/FDSI regional workshop report, Guwahati): "irregular stock updates"; discrepancies between state-reported data and the national dashboard; Jharkhand's AAM-SHC-level stock app live in only 2,533 of 3,900 facilities; Sikkim still tracking procurement in Google Sheets; explicit key takeaway from states — need for "offline-capable mobile applications with synchronization features" and full facility-level mapping. Warehouse logistics exist; last-mile visibility for the referring worker deciding whether to send a patient does not.

2.7 Paper registers + phone calls — the real incumbent

Zero infrastructure cost, universally understood, works during power cuts — beats every app on raw reliability in the worst conditions. It irreversibly loses on searchability, deduplication, aggregation, accountability trails, and anything at scale. Any honest design treats the paper register as the fallback mode and aims to replace it only where the app returns more value than it costs the worker in time.

3 · Innovation & USP (pressure-tested)

3.1 The structural bet

The platform's first-class data structure is a referral-with-SLA state machine: every encounter, teleconsultation, referral transition, escalation acknowledgment, follow-up outcome, and stock check writes into one cross-tier loop. Referral completion stops being an accident and becomes the measurable, escalatable product. This creates a dataset — end-to-end referral lifecycle telemetry across SC→PHC→CHC→DH — that no single-tier incumbent can generate, and which compounds: every tracked referral improves routing, staffing, and stocking decisions downstream.

3.2 Why someone picks this over each alternative (no marketing language)

  • Over eSanjeevani: peer-reviewed evidence names exactly what it lacks — structured triage, standardized referrals, feedback loops, continuity. We do not compete for its doctors; we supply its missing frontline layer and hand off hub consults to it. A district adopting this strengthens its existing eSanjeevani ROI rather than duplicating it.
  • Over Khushi Baby CHIP: open-source reference architecture vs state-tied NGO deployment; referral SLA + escalation + stock visibility as integrated core vs program-wise data collection. Deployable by any state/NHM team without vendor dependency.
  • Over Kilkari: reminders become two-way tasks tied to clinical reality (missed visit ⇒ different action), with closure signals returning to workers — not fixed-stage broadcasts.
  • Over DVDMS: not another warehouse LMIS; a read-visibility layer plus lightweight facility-side capture that feeds referring decisions, designed to import DVDMS where present (the exact "offline-capable sync" gap NHSRC records).
  • Over paper: replaces the register only when the app gives the ASHA back more time than it takes — caseload view, auto-referrals, incentive-relevant records — with paper as declared fallback, not a shamed relic.

3.3 USP statement

Unlike eSanjeevani (consultation-only, isolated events), Kilkari (one-way IVR education), or Khushi Baby (program-data collection per state deployment), this system is the cross-tier referral-and-follow-up state machine every level writes into — making referral completion itself the auditable, escalatable product and creating a cross-tier lifecycle dataset structurally unavailable inside any single-tier incumbent.

3.4 Strategic honesty for the presentation round

Near-identical concepts have appeared at SIH before (e.g., SIH25219, 2025: offline-first ASHA EHR companion). Judges will have seen this shape. Differentiation will come from three demonstrable things: (1) the working referral state machine with SLA clocks and acknowledged escalation — live, not slideware; (2) protocol depth — versioned IMCI/WHO-PEN rule bundles producing explainable scores with visible rule traces; (3) an honest integration story — eSanjeevani handoff and ABDM sandbox adapters shown as interfaces, with production-certification steps named rather than hand-waved.

4.1 Teleconsultation channel ladder

TierConditionMechanics
1 · VideoStable 3G+ detected at session startWebRTC peer call, CHO/ASHA-facilitated; auto-degrades down on packet loss
2 · Audio-onlyVoice-grade link onlyVoice call w/ vitals relayed verbally + typed by facilitator
3 · Store-and-forward (default)Intermittent connectivityWorker uploads photos/vitals/history; queued to doctor console; specialist reviews within SLA (e.g., 24 h routine / 4 h urgent); response returned as local-language IVR voice note + prescription to PHC
4 · In-person escalationRed-flag score or async review says soPriority referral created directly in state machine with shortest SLA

Rationale: PMC11422547 documents that real-world eSanjeevani quality suffers when connectivity assumptions fail mid-call; store-and-forward matches specialist availability reality instead of fighting it. Trade-off accepted: asynchronous tier sacrifices immediacy (hours, not minutes) — acceptable for non-red-flag cases by definition.

4.2 Component justifications (why separated)

  • Sync Service separate from Gateway: bursty fan-in (200 devices hitting block-office Wi-Fi simultaneously) has a distinct profile — long chunked uploads, retry storms — isolating it prevents API latency contamination; scales independently.
  • FHIR Store as dedicated service: keeps clinical semantics canonical and lets the same store serve future HIP certification without re-platforming; partitioning by region keeps queries district-local.
  • Rules Engine versioned and bundled: triage must work fully offline (rules ship with app builds); version pinning means every historical score can be reproduced — audit requirement, not nicety.
  • State machine + Follow-Up engine split: referrals are event-driven with SLA timers; enrollments are schedule-driven calendars; conflating them produced the "one-way handoff" anti-pattern the problem statement forbids.
  • Notification Adapter: Twilio is non-compliant-for-production in India without TRAI DLT registration and ~2× costlier; abstraction keeps provider swappable so pilots don't burn quota on the wrong rail.

4.4 Operational reality

  • Observability: per-device sync-lag metric, crash reporting, dead-letter queue inspection tooling for failed SMS/IVR, weekly district digest email generated from materialized views.
  • Debugging field issues: SyncJournal replays let support reproduce any device's exact op sequence server-side.
  • Rollback strategy: feature flags per facility; expand-contract DB migrations; nightly NDJSON export guarantees data portability away from the platform (lock-in insurance for government buyers).
  • Cost sketch (pilot district, order-of-magnitude): 1–2 app servers + managed PostgreSQL + object storage ≈ ₹15–25k/month; messaging at ~100k SMS/month ≈ ₹12–15k via DLT gateway; dominant cost is field training/change management, not compute.

5.1 Identity strategy

Progressive identity: the system runs fully on local IDs; ABHA linking happens opportunistically when connectivity allows (OTP flow fails offline — documented ABDM reality). Dedup uses name+fuzzy-DOB+village matching with worker confirmation UI. This keeps the hackathon build honest while the ABDM adapter remains a clean interface for post-pilot certification.

5.2 Consistency model

  • Clinical observations: append-only. Two workers' entries never conflict because nothing is mutated — corrections are new entries referencing the old.
  • Operational entities (stock counts, contact details): field-level last-writer-wins with server-assigned monotonic sequence per device; rare concurrent-edit loss accepted and flagged in trade-offs (below).
  • Referral transitions: single-writer state machine server-side; devices enqueue intents, server serializes — no distributed locking needed.

6 · Key Flows

6.1 Triage → route → refer → close (the loop)

  1. ASHA opens caseload → patient tile pre-filled from last encounter → symptom checklist (icons + voice prompts, Hindi/Marathi/…)
  2. Offline rules engine scores against versioned IMCI/WHO-PEN bundle → shows score + human-readable rule trace ("BP 160/+ swelling → red flag")
  3. Route decision rendered as action cards: self-care advice | PHC slot booking | teleconsult | emergency alert (location + summary one-tap)
  4. If referred: Referral(initiated, priority, sla_deadline) created locally → queued at emergency/referral priority class
  5. Sync on next connectivity window (jittered backoff, chunked upload)
  6. Receiving PHC sees referral inbox → taps Accept → accepted; slot confirmed to patient via SMS/IVR
  7. Visit logged at destination → completed; if SLA breached first: escalation ladder fires — referring ANM → block MO → district nodal officer, each hop requires tap-to-acknowledge; unacknowledged items render red on district dashboard
  8. Outcome (e.g., BP ≥140 confirmed) auto-enrolls patient into NCD protocol cohort → FollowUpTasks scheduled → adherence recorded back into the record → dashboard aggregates

6.2 Referral state machine

initiated ──▶ accepted ──▶ scheduled ──▶ completed
   │          │            │
   └─ SLA breach ─▶ ESCALATED(ack-required) ◀┘  (any state) ─▶ lapsed

SLA defaults: red-flag 24 h · urgent 48 h · routine 7 d — configurable per district. Every transition stamps actor, timestamp, and channel; the ladder is data, not email.

6.3 Sync protocol

  1. All mutations append to device SyncJournal with priority class (E>R>Enc>Analytics)
  2. On connectivity: jittered backoff schedules upload (avoids 200-device thundering herd at block office)
  3. Chunked batch upload in priority order; server validates, assigns sequence numbers, applies merge rules (§5.2), returns acks
  4. Delta download: only the worker's active caseload subset + updated rules bundle + referral inbox changes
  5. Failures park in dead-letter queue with per-device sync-lag surfaced on dashboards — silence is treated as an incident, not a state

6.4 Async teleconsultation (store-and-forward default)

  1. Worker captures photos/vitals/history offline → encrypted queue upload when connected
  2. Doctor console presents case card w/ longitudinal context (prior encounters, meds, referral history)
  3. Specialist reviews within SLA → structured response: prescription to PHC + local-language voice note routed via IVR to patient + advice note back to worker (the feedback loop eSanjeevani lacks)
  4. Consult outcome appended to record; red-flag findings spawn immediate in-person referral in the state machine

7.1 Trade-offs consciously accepted

  • Async teleconsult latency: specialist replies arrive in hours, not minutes — matched to specialist availability reality, but red-flag cases bypass via ladder tier 4.
  • Field-level LWW: rare concurrent edit of the same mutable field can drop one update — mitigated by making everything clinically meaningful append-only.
  • On-device minimization: workers lose full-history access offline (active caseload only) — deliberate privacy-for-convenience exchange; full history retrievable on demand when online.
  • Rule-based (not ML) triage: lower ceiling, fully explainable and auditable offline — correct trade for a safety-critical frontline decision under DPDP scrutiny.

8 · Summary & Open Questions

Problem

Rural public healthcare fails not from missing services but from absent cross-tier accountability — referrals go untracked, follow-ups lapse silently, and no actor can see (or be held responsible for) care falling through the cracks between sub-centre and district hospital. Every incumbent system (§2) is a vertical silo; the cross-tier closure quadrant is empty.

Architecture (one paragraph)

Offline-first Flutter field app (SQLCipher, icon triage <90 s, Bluetooth vitals) and pharmacist web-lite, syncing via priority-classed jittered delta journal into a Node.js gateway; HAPI FHIR R4/PostgreSQL longitudinal store; versioned IMCI/WHO-PEN offline rules engine producing explainable triage scores; tiered teleconsultation (video→audio→store-and-forward default→in-person) with eSanjeevani handoff; server-side referral state machine with SLA clocks and acknowledgment-required escalation ladders; DLT-compliant SMS/IVR notification abstraction; enrollment-driven follow-up engine writing adherence back to the record; read-replica district dashboards with anomaly detection; ABDM sandbox HIP adapter behind a clean interface, local-ID-first identity.

USP

The referral-with-SLA state machine is the first-class data structure every tier writes into — unlike eSanjeevani (isolated consultation events, documented absence of triage structure and feedback loops), Kilkari (stage-driven one-way IVR), or Khushi Baby (state-tied program-data collection) — making referral completion itself the auditable product and yielding a cross-tier lifecycle dataset no single-tier incumbent can structurally generate.

Open questions requiring decisions before build

  1. Pilot state/district choice — determines which DVDMS variant (UP e-Aushadhi vs central DVDMS vs Assam-style AAM app) the stock import adapter must speak first.
  2. Demo doctor coverage — staff our own console with volunteer MBBS students for the pilot, or stub eSanjeevani handoff? Affects teleconsult realism in evaluation rounds.
  3. Minimum device floor — commit to Android Go / 1 GB RAM? Constrains Flutter app size, SQLCipher viability, and photo-capture UX.
  4. ABDM sandbox registration timing — feasible before your presentation round? If not, the adapter ships with mock credentials and the certification path stays documentation-only.
  5. Language set for v1 — pick 2 (e.g., Hindi + one regional per pilot district) rather than promising many.
